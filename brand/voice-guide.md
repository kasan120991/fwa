# Francis Web Agency — Voice Guide

**Companion to** `readme.md` → *Content Fundamentals*. That section defines the voice; this
document is the working playbook — the specific moves, examples, and guardrails for writing
marketing and prose copy in it.

Origin note: this guide folds in techniques admired from a peer agency's copy
(empathy-first openings, plain-language honesty, specificity as proof). It keeps FWA's
established register and **rejects the moves that conflict with it** — self-deprecation and
profanity chief among them. See *Calibration* below.

---

## The voice in one line

**A sharp, plain-spoken peer who clearly knows the craft — leads with your problem, backs every
claim with a specific, and tells you the truth even when it costs the sale.**

Personality rides on top of competence, never instead of it. Every relatable line is still
anchored to a concrete claim.

---

## The four pillars (unchanged — this is who we already are)

1. **Professional** — credibility comes from concrete detail ("loads in under a second," "built to
   handle 10× your traffic"), not adjectives.
2. **Friendly** — write like a helpful person, not a brochure. Contractions, plain words, "you."
   Warmth lives in the language, not in exclamation points.
3. **Business savvy** — lead with outcomes (leads, credibility, growth, measurable results).
   Respect the reader's time and budget.
4. **Modern & human** — plain-spoken, a little dry wit, zero corporate buzzwords. A capable peer,
   not a vendor or a hype account.

---

## What we're adopting (the moves worth borrowing)

These sharpen the four pillars — they don't replace them.

### 1. Open on the reader's problem, in their own words
Lead every page with the reader's situation, not FWA's résumé. Name the frustration they'd
recognize before you introduce yourself.

- **Do:** "Your website should be pulling its weight. Most don't."
- **Not:** "Francis Web Agency is a full-service web design studio based in Valdosta, GA."

### 2. Make plain language an explicit promise
The reader doesn't need to know what a CMS is — that's our job. Say so, and then prove it by never
making them look anything up.

- **Do:** "You don't need to know the difference between hosting and a domain. That's what you're
  hiring me for."

### 3. Honesty as the differentiator
Radical candor is a positioning, not a phrase. We say when an idea won't work, when a fix beats a
rebuild, when the cheaper option is the right one. It reads as trustworthy *because* most agencies
over-sell.

- **Do:** "If a feature is going to cost you money and confuse your customers, I'll say so — before
  you pay for it, not after."

### 4. Specificity is the proof
Numbers, dates, and concrete details do the persuading. Replace every "high-quality" and
"results-driven" with something measurable.

- **Do:** "Sub-one-second load times." / "Built to handle 10× your traffic." / "Live in 4–6 weeks."
- **Not:** "Fast, reliable, high-performance websites."

### 5. Lean on the solo advantage
FWA is one person — the one who sells the site is the one who builds it. That's the thing a bigger
agency literally cannot say. Use it: direct access, no hand-offs, no telephone game, no markup for
an account manager.

- **Do:** "When you email, I answer. The person you talked to is the person doing the work."

## Signature techniques (the "how")

- **Problem → promise structure.** State the reader's pain, then the plain-language fix. Most
  sections can run on this two-beat rhythm.
- **Brag, then ground it — don't undercut it.** Claims are fine; back them with a specific instead
  of a joke at your own expense. "38 sites a year" earns its keep with the number, not with
  "apparently people like us."
- **Rhetorical question → tight answer.** Sparingly. "Need a rebuild or just a refresh? Usually a
  refresh. I'll tell you which after I look."
- **Dry wit, landed once.** One wry line per section, max. Wit seasons; it isn't the meal.
- **The em dash for the honest aside** — it's our punctuation for the plain-spoken clarification,
  the "here's the real story" beat.
- **"you" carries the copy; "we"/"I" answers it.** Address the reader far more than we talk about
  ourselves.

## Calibration — where we deliberately differ from the voice we admired

The admired source is very folksy and leans on mild profanity and self-deprecation. FWA looks
polished (ink on paper, bold Geist, a single citrine stroke, generous whitespace). If we *talked* folksy, the voice would
fight the visual identity. We keep the **posture** (empathy, plainness, honesty, specificity) and
raise the **register**:

| The admired source does… | FWA does instead |
| --- | --- |
| Mild profanity ("suck," "freakin'") | Dry, grown-up wit. No profanity. |
| Self-deprecation ("Proof We Don't Suck") | Confidence, grounded in specifics. We never undercut the work. |
| Folksy asides, lots of them | One wry line per section; clarity leads. |
| Exclamation energy | Warmth in word choice, not punctuation. |
| "Awesome folks," pet mascots, "family" | Peer-to-peer respect. Warm, not cutesy. |

**The test:** the copy should sound like it *looks* — confident, clean, a little dry. If a line
would feel wrong set in Geist 700 next to a citrine sweep, rewrite it.

## Do / Don't

**Do**
- Start with the reader's problem.
- Replace adjectives with numbers.
- Say the honest thing, even when it's not the upsell.
- Use contractions and "you."
- Cut any word the sentence survives without.

**Don't**
- Open with FWA's credentials or location.
- Use corporate filler: *leverage, seamless, unlock, empower, cutting-edge, best-in-class,
  synergistic, simply, just.*
- Undercut the work with self-deprecation.
- Reach for internet/meme voice, slang, or emoji.
- Pile on wit — one landed line beats three that don't.

---

## Words we don't use

leverage · seamless · unlock · empower · cutting-edge · best-in-class · synergy/synergistic ·
simply · just · game-changer · robust · solutions (as a catch-all) · world-class

Rule of thumb: **if the sentence still makes sense with a word removed, remove it.**

## Before / after

**Hero**
- ❌ "Welcome to Francis Web Agency, your full-service partner for cutting-edge web solutions."
- ✅ "Your website should be pulling its weight. Most don't. Let's fix that."

**Service intro**
- ❌ "We leverage best-in-class technology to deliver seamless, high-performance websites."
- ✅ "Fast, clean sites built to load in under a second — because a slow page is a lost customer."

**Differentiator**
- ❌ "We pride ourselves on exceptional communication and customer service."
- ✅ "When you email, I answer. No account manager, no hand-off, no wondering who's actually
  building your site."

**Close**
- ❌ "Contact us today to unlock your online potential!"
- ✅ "Tell me where things stand, and I'll tell you honestly whether I can help — and what it'd
  take."

## 30-second checklist before copy ships

1. Does it open on the reader, not on us?
2. Is every claim backed by something concrete?
3. Did I say the honest thing, or the easy sell?
4. Is there exactly one wry line here — not zero, not four?
5. Would it feel at home in Geist 700 next to a citrine sweep?
6. Can I delete any word and keep the meaning? (Then delete it.)
